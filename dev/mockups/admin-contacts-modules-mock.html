1A · Trust spectrum — the segmented strip (picked)

One strip across the top of the Directory: Unknown sits apart on the left (it isn't a level of trust, it means "not decided"), then Suspicious → Implicitly trusted, each band as wide as how many contacts are in it, with a dot per contact in its role colour. Click bands to filter the list underneath (several at once); hover a band to read the names. Trust as seen by switches the strip to one character's own trust — counts, dots and the list all re-sort, and only the contacts that character knows are counted. A violet ring on a dot, and the line on a row, mean some character trusts that contact differently from the default.

1B · Trust spectrum — lanes by role, with each character's spread (not picked, kept as a record)

The same scale as columns, but one lane per role, so the shape answers "who do we trust, by kind of person" — fixers lean trusted, corpos lean suspicious. Each dot is a contact at its default trust; a violet line behind a dot runs from the lowest to the highest trust any character holds, so disagreement is visible without opening anyone. Hover a dot to see each character's trust as lettered pips on the line. Click a column, a role, or a dot to narrow the list; "Only where characters disagree" keeps just the contacts with a line.

2A · Who knows whom — the constellation (picked; first pass, compare with 2A-cyber below)

The five player characters are the bright nodes around the edge. A contact sits between the characters who know them: known by one = just outside that character, shared by two = between them, known by everyone = in the middle. Lines show "knows". Names stay hidden until you focus — hover or click a character to light up their contacts and name them, or a contact to see who knows them. The strip under the map is where you act: share with a character (dashed) or take back (solid). Contacts nobody knows sit in the dock below, not on the map. Highlight chips pick out a group ("The Crew", dashed violet ring) or the shared/lonely contacts.

2A-cyber · Who knows whom — the netmap (picked direction, second pass)

Same layout, same rules, same actions as 2A above — redrawn as a net map. Characters are hex chips with a name plate; contacts are diamond nodes in their role colour (size = how many characters know them, dashed violet frame = in the highlighted group). Links are circuit traces: a 45° run out of the contact, then straight into the character, so the lines arrive at each chip as a bus instead of a fan. Idle traces sit dim; focusing a character or contact lights its traces with a soft glow and puts every name on its own backplate, placed so no name covers a node, another name, or a chip. A grid and a slow scan line sit behind everything (the scan stops under reduced motion). The mono readout strip above the map counts the graph and, on focus, the focused node. Click a chip or a node to open the information panel under the map (it slides in): for a contact — tags, address, trust, who knows them with share / take back per character, groups, last mail and the actions; for a character — player, how many they know, who only they know, groups they hold, and The Crew members they're missing. With nothing picked the panel is a legend of the shapes and lines.

2A-cyber-grid · the netmap routed on the grid (variant)

Same map, traces drawn as a circuit board: the grid is 10px (a major line every 50px) and every trace runs along a grid line or a grid diagonal — every bend lands on an intersection. When several traces arrive at a chip from the same side they take adjacent grid lines, one wire per line. Nodes are snapped to the nearest grid point; the furthest any node moves is 7px, which changes nothing the layout says (who sits between whom), so the snap is kept. Compare with 2A-cyber above, where the traces are free and only their bends sit on the 45°.

2B · Who knows whom — ring of rings (not picked, kept as a record)

The matrix bent into a circle, so nothing overlaps: every contact is a spoke (grouped by role, colour band on the outside, name at the end), every character is a ring (V innermost, Brick outermost). A filled cell = that character knows that contact; a ring with gaps is a character with blind spots, a spoke filled all the way is someone everyone knows. Click a ring label to see one character, a name or a cell to see one contact; the card on the right shows the numbers and does the share / take back. "Colour by trust" re-paints the filled cells with each character's own trust in that contact.
