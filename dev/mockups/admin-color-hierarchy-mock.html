The hierarchy

What changes

The background ladder has real steps, one job each. Canvas (behind everything) → pane (the list) → pane-alt (the reading pane, deliberately a different shade, so the two halves of every split read as two things) → card head (group headers and section bands) → hover → raised (popups). In clean-dark the old ladder moved a few steps between levels; the new one moves enough to see.

Four text tiers. Headings (names, subjects, titles) are the brightest and heavier; body is the next step; labels (small caps, section names, field keys) are mid; hints and metadata are dim. Before, headings and body were the same colour, so a list of mail had no anchor.

One colour, one meaning — everywhere. Cyan = you can click it / it's selected (the selected row, the active tab, the primary button, links). Amber = needs you (waiting mail, the unread dot, warm heat, "needs a look"). Red = wrong or dangerous (failures, ICE, lockouts, self-destruct). Green = fine. Gold = money only. Violet = hidden or secret (anonymous, aliases, encrypted, countermeasures). Before, gold meant warning AND money AND accent, and red meant danger AND the brand.

Decoration goes neutral. Section icons, tab icons and group-header icons drop to the label tone; only the active tab's icon keeps the accent. Plain-fact flags (Group ×2, via a terminal) are neutral too — they are information, not a warning. Colour is left for the things that need your eye.

How it ships

The tokens go into admin-theme.css for all three presets (the cv-* names here map onto the --ncm-* names the tabs already read, so most of it lands with no tab changes). The thin "meaning" layer — panes at different levels, selected = tint + edge, flags by meaning, neutral decoration — moves into each tab's CSS. The player-facing windows are untouched: this is the admin theme only.

Contrast: the sheet measures every text tier and meaning colour against the pane it sits on (WCAG ratio, live). Text tiers aim ≥ 4.5 (hints included), meaning colours ≥ 3 as chips; anything under is shown in red.
