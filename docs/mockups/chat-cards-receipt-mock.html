Direction B · Agent printouts

The idea

These cards are artifacts, not panels: a message someone forwarded to the table, a roll's arithmetic, a security hit. In Blade Runner those are printouts — the Esper photo, the police ticket, the Voight-Kampff strip — and in Cyberpunk RED they're screamsheets and Data Term slips. The chat log already spools like paper. So every card is a thermal slip from your Agent's printer: torn top and bottom, one ink, a rubber stamp where a verdict matters, a barcode for the id.

Why it reads better

Dark ink on pale paper is the most legible thing you can put in a 300px column, and nothing else in Foundry or in NCM looks like it. Everything printed is one size, one mono face (Share Tech Mono at 12.5px — a printer has one font); only the subject and the stamps use Rajdhani. There are no labels in caps, no accent bar, no scanlines: a receipt's grammar is key … dotted leader … value, and a roll is an itemised bill that sums to the total.

Cool thermal grey-white, not cream. Ink and faded ink. Two stamp inks: red for warnings and failures, blue for passes. Nothing else. The slip never changes colour except the citation, where the whole print turns red ink.

What each card does

Shared message — printer header says whose Agent printed it and the line; From/To with portraits printed in halftone grey; subject; body (a thermal printer has no bold, so emphasis is underlined); an Add contact button for people not in your list; URGENT is a stamp. Long messages fold at a cut line (✂ "cut here · 9 more lines").
Skill check — the bill: TECH 7, Interface 4, d10 8, total 19, DV 15, then the verdict in words and a PASSED / FAILED stamp. A critical prints a "10 → 17 (explodes)" line; a fumble "1 → −6".
Black ICE — a citation: the whole slip prints in red ink, damage as the big number.
Malware analysis — the slip is overprinted GM EYES ONLY, since it is a whisper.
Broadcast — a public notice from the network operator, stamped PUBLIC.
Trace warning — a GM whisper, same overprint.

Themeable?

Deliberately not. Paper is paper. The player theme reaches the ink only (a dark player theme could invert the slip to a phosphor-green readout — that's the one knob I'd offer: chatCardStyle: paper | phosphor).

If this isn't it

The other direction I'd try is the Esper readout: dark, but built like the enhance screen — a wide tracked record line across the top, the verdict as one enormous numeral, data in a strip of blocky seven-segment digits, no borders at all. Say which, or neither.
