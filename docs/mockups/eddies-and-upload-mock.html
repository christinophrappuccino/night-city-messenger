② UPLOAD / FORGE — the missing direction (concept · not built)

Today every content action flows terminal → player: siphon, exfil, claim, save-frame. You take things. Upload is the inverse — player → terminal — and it's the whole netrunner fantasy: get in, leave something behind. The big realization: almost all of it already exists. The composer authors 18 content types; payloads already carry module-native effects (icebreaker / jammer / scanner…); shards already carry typed entries as a go-bag; the SecurityConsole already runs skill-gated actions with trace-on-fail. Upload is mostly composition, not new systems — the new parts are "carry it" + "push it onto a machine you're in" + a skill check to do it cleanly.

Two families, straight from your two examples:

PLANT (social / forgery) — the payload is content: a forged chatlog, a doctored gallery, a fake bank statement, a fabricated dossier. Upload it and it becomes a real entry the next person to log in finds. The "wife cheating" / blackmail case. This is almost free — the authoring pipeline already makes all 18 types; the new bit is a FORGE check (a botched forgery leaves tells a sharp GM/player can catch).

EXECUTE (offensive) — the payload is a program that does something on upload: wipe a log, jam the uplink, unlock/forge an account, or lie in wait as a trap. This is the payload effect engine, pointed at a host you don't own — malware and payload are converging (utility program vs hostile program, same machinery). A "malware creator" = the composer's malware form + an authored-effects menu (like payload already has).

My recommended path: Phase 1 = PLANT (cheapest, delivers the blackmail fantasy immediately — carry a content entry, upload it, forge check).
